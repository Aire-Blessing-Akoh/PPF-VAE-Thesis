METHODOLOGY NOTE (added after initial publication): the original FGSM/PGD attack implementation
perturbed each sample to MAXIMIZE reconstruction error, which is backwards for a genuine evasion
threat model (a real adversary wants to MINIMIZE reconstruction error so malicious traffic is
misclassified as benign). Experiment 1 and Experiment 4 below have been re-evaluated with the
attack direction corrected (x_adv = x - epsilon * sign(grad)). Experiment 2 and Experiment 3 below
still reflect the ORIGINAL, UNCORRECTED attack direction and have NOT been re-verified -- treat
their numbers as unreliable pending re-evaluation.

SUMMARY: Under the corrected (genuine-evasion) attack direction, PPF-VAE (Experiment 4) is
roughly THREE TIMES MORE VULNERABLE than the non-private Centralized VAE (Experiment 1) at the
strongest tested FGSM perturbation (13.38 pp accuracy drop / 16.21% attack success rate, vs. 4.40 pp
/ 4.40% for Experiment 1). This reverses the direction originally reported. Diagnostic analysis of
the clean reconstruction-error distributions shows that PPF-VAE's anomaly-detection threshold on
this dataset sits in a more densely populated region of the malicious-sample error distribution
than the Centralized VAE's does, leaving a larger share of its correctly-detected malicious samples
only marginally above the decision boundary and therefore easier to evade with a small
gradient-based perturbation. Compare against CICIOT23/adversarial_comparison.txt, where the
corrected-direction result shows the opposite pattern (PPF-VAE genuinely more robust) -- the effect
of DP-SGD on adversarial robustness is dataset-dependent, not a universal co-benefit.
